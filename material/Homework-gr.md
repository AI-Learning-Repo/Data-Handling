# Group Homework: Situational Awareness — Looking Back from Today

## Purpose

The purpose of this activity is to discuss different perspectives on the future of artificial intelligence and to practice evaluating predictions about rapidly changing technologies.

You will work in the groups formed during the first lecture. If you were not able to join a group, you may complete the activity individually.

---

## Reading

Read (or carefully review the main arguments of):

**Leopold Aschenbrenner, *Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead* (2024)**

The essay was written in 2024 and argues that Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) could arrive around 2027, followed by a rapid transition toward Artificial Superintelligence (ASI).

**Reading:** *Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead*

https://situational-awareness.ai/

You do not need to agree with the author. The goal is to understand the argument and critically evaluate it.

---

# Your Task

Prepare a short presentation (**5–7 slides**) to present during the next class.

Your presentation should answer the following questions.

## 1. What is the essay arguing?

Briefly summarize the author's main ideas.

For example:

* Why does the author believe AGI could arrive soon?
* What evidence does he use?
* What does he expect to happen after AGI?

Keep this section brief (about 1 slide).

---

## 2. Looking back from today: What was right and what was wrong?

The essay was written in **2024**.

Looking at developments since then:

* Which predictions seem to have been accurate?
* Which predictions have not happened yet?
* Which predictions now seem less likely or more uncertain?
* Were there important developments that the author did not emphasize?

Try to distinguish between:

* **Predictions that have been supported by events**
* **Predictions that remain unresolved**
* **Predictions that appear to have been too optimistic or too pessimistic**

---

## 3. Are we close to AGI?

Discuss your group's view.

* Do you believe AGI is close?
* What would count as convincing evidence that AGI has been achieved?
* Are today's AI systems already showing some general capabilities, or are they still fundamentally limited?

There is no single correct answer. Explain the reasoning behind your view.

---

## 4. What about ASI?

The essay argues that once AGI is achieved, progress toward Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) could happen very quickly.

Discuss:

* Do you find this argument convincing?
* Do you share the author's concerns about ASI?
* What do you think are the biggest potential benefits?
* What do you think are the biggest potential risks?

---

## 5. If you were updating the essay today...

Imagine that you were asked to publish a new version of *Situational Awareness* today.

What would you change?

Consider questions such as:

* Which arguments would you keep?
* Which arguments would you weaken or remove?
* What new developments would you emphasize?
* What do you think the next 3–5 years of AI development will look like?

---

# Presentation Requirements

Prepare **5–7 slides**.

A suggested structure is:

| Slide | Content                                     |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | Title, group members, and your overall view |
| **2** | Main arguments of the essay                 |
| **3** | What the author got right                   |
| **4** | What did not happen or remains uncertain    |
| **5** | Your view on AGI                            |
| **6** | Your view on ASI                            |
| **7** | How you would update the essay today        |

---

# Discussion Guidelines

* There is **no expected position** on whether AGI or ASI is near.
* Your group members may disagree.
* Try to support your opinions with examples, evidence, or clear reasoning.
* Clearly distinguish between **the author's views** and **your group's views**.

---

# Submission

* Work in your course group.
* If you could not form a group, complete the activity individually.
* Bring your slides to the next session.
* Be prepared to give a **5–7 minute presentation** followed by a short class discussion.

---

## Learning Goal

This activity is not about predicting the future correctly. It is about learning to critically evaluate claims about AI progress, compare forecasts with evidence, and discuss the technical and societal implications of increasingly capable AI systems.
