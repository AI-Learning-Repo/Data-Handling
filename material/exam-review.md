# Exam Question Design and Review

## Purpose

This activity is part of the exam preparation for Weeks 2–5.

Rather than providing a fixed list of questions for revision, you will contribute to building a **question bank for the exam**. The purpose is twofold:

* to review the main concepts and learning objectives from the assigned week;
* to practise identifying what constitutes a relevant and meaningful assessment question.

This approach assumes that, after working with the material, you are capable of identifying the concepts and skills that should be assessed.

**At least 50% of the final exam questions will be based on the questions selected through this process.** Questions may be used directly or adapted where necessary.

---

# Stage 1: Pair work

Each group consists of four students and will be divided into two pairs.

Each pair will be assigned one of Weeks 2–5.

**Time: approximately 45–60 minutes**

For your assigned week:

1. Review the relevant material and identify the main concepts, methods, and learning objectives.
2. Find or formulate potential exam questions that assess these areas.
3. Select the questions that you consider most relevant.
4. Prepare a short justification for each selected question.

The aim is **not to collect as many questions as possible**. Focus on questions that provide meaningful coverage of the material.

### For each proposed question, provide:

**Question**
The proposed exam question.

**Topic / concept**
Which main concept or learning objective does it assess?

**Rationale**
Why is this an appropriate question for assessing the material?

**Expected answer**
Briefly indicate the key elements that a correct answer should contain.

**Difficulty**
Easy / Medium / Challenging

**Source**
If the question is adapted from an external source, provide the source.

---

# Criteria for selecting questions

Consider the following when evaluating a question:

* Does it address an important concept or learning objective?
* Does it assess meaningful understanding rather than an incidental detail?
* Is the question sufficiently clear and unambiguous?
* Is it possible to determine what constitutes a correct or strong answer?
* Does it assess an appropriate level of understanding for the course?
* Does it add something that is not already covered by another question?

Where appropriate, include different levels of difficulty and different types of reasoning, such as explanation, application, comparison, analysis, or problem solving.

---

# Stage 2: Consolidation

After Stage 1, pairs working on the same week will be brought together.

For example, all pairs assigned to Week 3 will form a larger review group.

The group will:

1. Combine the questions produced by the different pairs.
2. Identify duplicate or substantially overlapping questions.
3. Evaluate the relevance and quality of the questions.
4. Remove questions that focus on minor or inappropriate material.
5. Improve unclear or poorly formulated questions.
6. Check that the question set provides reasonable coverage of the main concepts.
7. Review the expected answers.
8. Produce a final set of questions for the week.

The final question set will form part of the **exam question pool**.

---

## Outcome

The result of this activity will be a consolidated question bank covering Weeks 2–5.

The exam will use **at least 50% of its questions from this student-generated question bank**, either directly or with minor modifications.

The objective is therefore not simply to predict the exam. It is to review the material by considering **which knowledge and skills are important enough to assess and how they can be assessed effectively**.
