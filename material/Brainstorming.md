# Group Activity: Brainstorming the Mini Project Domain

## The Mini Project

Starting next week, you will work in groups on a collaborative **AI mini project**.

The project will be developed using **Scrum** and will be completed through **two sprints**.

---

## Sprint 1 — Fine-Tuned Model

In the first sprint, your group will:

* Identify a problem and a suitable domain.
* Find and explore an appropriate dataset.
* Prepare and curate the data.
* Create a training dataset.
* Fine-tune a **Qwen** model.
* Save and use the fine-tuned model.
* Evaluate the results.
* Present your work.

Possible domains include:

* **Healthcare**
* **Finance**
* **Cybersecurity**
* **Education**
* **Law**
* **Science**
* **Customer support**
* **Journalism**
* **Business**
* Or another domain that your group finds interesting.

---

## Sprint 2 — RAG and Method Comparison

In the second sprint, you will extend the project and investigate a different approach to solving the same or a related problem.

You will:

* Create embeddings.
* Work with a vector database.
* Implement a **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** workflow.
* Evaluate retrieval and generated results.
* Compare **RAG with fine-tuning**.
* Compare modern approaches with **TF-IDF**.
* Discuss which approach is most suitable for your problem and why.

The important part is the **comparison**.

You should be able to explain:

> **Why does this approach work better or worse for this particular problem and dataset?**

---

# Today's Activity: Where Should Our Project Be?

Today, your task is **not to choose the final project**.

Instead, your group will brainstorm possible domains and problems that could work well for the two-sprint project.

The goal is to leave the session with **2–3 promising ideas** that you can investigate further.

---

## Step 1 — Choose Areas That Interest You

Start by discussing different domains.

For example:

* Healthcare
* Finance
* Cybersecurity
* Law
* Education
* Journalism
* Science
* Business / customer support
* Environment
* Entertainment
* Or something else

Ask each group member:

> **Which domain would you be interested in working with for several weeks?**

You don't need to have technical knowledge of the domain already.

---

## Step 2 — Brainstorm Possible Problems

For each interesting domain, think about **specific problems** rather than just applications.

For example:

### Healthcare

Instead of:

> "Let's build an AI for healthcare."

Think:

> "Can we classify patient questions into different categories?"

or:

> "Can we summarize medical information from a specific collection of documents?"

### Finance

Instead of:

> "Let's build an AI for finance."

Think:

> "Can we classify financial news by topic or sentiment?"

or:

> "Can we retrieve and answer questions about financial reports?"

### Cybersecurity

Instead of:

> "Let's build an AI for cybersecurity."

Think:

> "Can we classify security incidents?"

or:

> "Can we identify the type of attack described in a security report?"

---

# Step 3 — Think About the Data

For each promising idea, ask:

### What data would we need?

* Is there a public dataset?
* Is the dataset large enough?
* Is the data in a suitable format?
* Can we legally use it?
* Does it contain enough examples for training?
* Could the same data also support a RAG experiment?

Remember that **data availability is one of the most important constraints**.

A great project idea without usable data may not be a practical project.

---

# Step 4 — Think About the Two Sprints

Your idea should work for **both parts of the project**.

Discuss:

### Sprint 1

Could we create a useful supervised training dataset and fine-tune Qwen 1B?

### Sprint 2

Could we also use relevant documents or knowledge to build a RAG system?

### Comparison

Could we meaningfully compare:

**Fine-tuning vs. RAG?**

This is important.

The project should give you something interesting to **compare**, not just something to build.

---

---

# Important: The Decision Is Not Final

Today's choice is a **starting point**, not a commitment.

Before Sprint 1 begins, your group will have time to:

* investigate datasets,
* refine the problem,
* check whether the data is suitable,
* consider ethical and practical issues,
* and decide whether the idea works for both fine-tuning and RAG.

It is completely acceptable to change your idea later.

---

## The Main Question

As you brainstorm, keep this question in mind:

> **What real problem could we investigate where the characteristics of the data might make different AI approaches perform differently?**

That question will become especially important in **Sprint 2**, when you compare fine-tuning, and RAG** and justify which approach is most appropriate for your problem.
